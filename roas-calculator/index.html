How to calculate ROAS

ROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend

If a campaign cost 1,000 and brought in 4,000 in revenue, ROAS = 4,000 ÷ 1,000 = 4. People write it as 4, 4× or 400%. Each means the same thing: every 1 spent on ads brought back 4 in revenue.

With 50 orders, the same campaign has an average order value of 4,000 ÷ 50 = 80 and a cost per order of 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20.

What is a good ROAS?

No single number is “good” for everyone. It depends on your margin. ROAS counts revenue, not profit, so you need to compare it with your break-even ROAS:

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Gross margin

With a 40% gross margin, break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.4 = 2.5. The campaign above, with a ROAS of 4, earns 4,000 × 40% = 1,600 in gross profit. After 1,000 of ad spend, that leaves 600 in profit. At a ROAS of 2, the same campaign would lose money. More on this in the break-even ROAS calculator.

FAQ

How do you calculate ROAS?

Divide the revenue from ads by the ad spend. 4,000 in revenue from 1,000 in ad spend is a ROAS of 4, often written as 4× or 400%.

What is a good ROAS?

There is no single good number. A ROAS is good when it is above your break-even ROAS, which is 1 divided by your gross margin. With a 40% margin you break even at a ROAS of 2.5; with a 70% margin, at about 1.43.

What is the difference between ROAS and ACOS?

They are the same ratio turned upside down. ROAS is revenue ÷ ad spend; ACOS is ad spend ÷ revenue. A ROAS of 4 equals an ACOS of 25%.

Is ROAS the same as ROI?

No. ROAS uses revenue, ROI uses profit. ROAS does not account for what the goods cost you, so a campaign with a ROAS of 2 can still lose money if your margin is below 50%.
