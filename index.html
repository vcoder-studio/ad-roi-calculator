How the reverse calculation works

Say you need 2,000 in profit from ads next month. You know your average order is 120, your gross margin is 50%, a click costs 1.20 and 3% of clicks buy.

  1. Profit from one sale before ads: 120 × 50% = 60.
  2. Ad cost of one sale: 1.20 ÷ 3% = 40.
  3. Profit per sale after ads: 60 − 40 = 20.
  4. Sales needed: 2,000 ÷ 20 = 100 sales.
  5. Clicks needed: 100 ÷ 3% ≈ 3,334 clicks.
  6. Ad budget: 100 × 40 = 4,000. That gives 12,000 in revenue and a ROAS of 3.

FAQ

How do I calculate the ad budget I need for a profit goal?

Work out the gross profit one sale brings (average order value × gross margin) and what one sale costs in ads (cost per click ÷ conversion rate). The difference is the profit per sale. Divide your profit goal by it to get the number of sales, then multiply the sales by the ad cost per sale to get the budget.

What is the difference between ROAS and ROI?

ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend. It ignores what the goods cost you. ROI (also called ROMI here) is net profit divided by ad spend: revenue × gross margin − ad spend, divided by ad spend. A campaign can have a ROAS above 1 and still lose money.
